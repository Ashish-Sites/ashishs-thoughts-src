Modern digital publishing encourages certainty. We package our reflections as definitive listicles, bulletproof manifestos, and algorithmic conclusions. We feel an urge to tidy up every messy contradiction before hitting publish.

Yet, reading through Montaigne's original *Essais* or the private notebooks of Leonardo da Vinci, one is struck by the opposite virtue: the courage to wander without a pre-approved conclusion. The word *essay* itself originates from the French *essayer*—to try, to attempt, to test.

### Writing as an Instrument of Discovery

If you only write when you already know what you believe, writing becomes merely a clerical task of transcription. But when you write to explore the perimeter of your confusion, the sentence becomes a lantern.

```
Transcribing  -->  Known opinions arranged neatly
Essayistic    -->  Stumbling forward into unknown territory
```

### Embracing the Fragment

Consider keeping a section of your personal journal or blog dedicated to fragments:
* Hypotheses you are not yet ready to defend
* Contradictions in your own daily conduct
* Questions without obvious answers

Publishing these fragments invites dialogue rather than debate. It creates an opening for readers to think *alongside* you rather than merely evaluating your performance.
