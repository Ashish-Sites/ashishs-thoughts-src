Every platform where we publish our ideas promises permanence, right until the quarterly business model shifts or the company is acquired and quietly sunsetted.

Over the past two decades, we have watched blogs, social feeds, bookmarking services, and note-taking apps vanish into 404 dead ends. Millions of intimate essays, personal records, and spontaneous sparks of genius dissolved because they were trapped in proprietary databases behind OAuth walls.

### The Plain Text Principle

Plain text is durable. A file written in ASCII or UTF-8 markdown in 1986 can still be read without conversion by any computer on earth today, and it will remain readable two centuries from now. It requires no specialized proprietary renderer, no cloud subscription, and no active server daemon.

```markdown
# 2026-08-02: Observations
The simplicity of plain text is its durability.
No lock-in. No deprecation cycles. Pure thought.
```

When you store your personal essays as raw markdown files and version them using Git, you decouple your expression from any single vendor or hosting company.

### Why Git is the Writer's Best Friend

1. **Deterministic History**: Every revision, edit, deletion, and conceptual shift is cryptographically tracked with an immutable SHA.
2. **True Redundancy**: You have identical copies on your local machine, your server, and backup repositories on GitHub.
3. **Zero Lock-In**: If one platform goes dark tomorrow, you clone your repository elsewhere and rebuild your website in minutes.

The most radical architecture for the personal web is also the simplest: static markdown, semantic typography, and open version control. Own your words.
